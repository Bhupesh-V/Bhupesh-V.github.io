*Don’t hate the player, hate the game* is an idiom used a lot but never argued for or against. In the language I grew up with (hindi), another catchphrase, “*Apna kam banta, bhad me jaye janta*” (which roughly translates to: *My work is done, to hell with the public*) carries a similar cynical rhetorical weight, both expressions ask us to accept systemic flaws as inevitable. I wanna challenge this status quo by addressing questions like, Does being beneficiary of a system that benefits me deserve my respect & admiration? On what basis? What happens if the benefit goes away? What if I create that system?

To answer this, we need to be clear on what a “system" is, I have been dealing with this term for the past few months, following key characteristics make a system recognizable:

1. Composed of living and non-living components. When taken apart have no inherent meaning or use.  
2. Has an implicit or explicitly defined purpose. The implicit purpose can also align well with the [POSWID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_purpose_of_a_system_is_what_it_does) ideology.  
3. The components work together to achieve that purpose.

Based on these characteristics some examples of valid systems include religion, economic models, not-for-profit NGOs, a city-local bookclub, forms of government, human body etc. However things like sand on a beach and strangers watching a movie in a theatre are not valid systems.

Discrediting a system is difficult, especially when people have bankrupted themselves building businesses within it, died protecting its boundaries as a nation or brought essentials to modern human living standards. Why people defend systems is well argued in [psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850299/), a simple justification is accepting the game if you hold no power in remodeling it, so it's best to build around it, your identity, your beliefs, everything. Another most commonly cited reason is survival, conforming to a group or system is considered much safer and increases your chance to get socially accepted.

Fair assessment, however, I think we can choose to not believe in borders, yet understand that some humans can act in subjectively evil ways and ruin the history of a well documented culture. However, I will even go as far as to say that cultures aren't meant to be protected, they aren’t your [friend](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0JneoZwQ_E), they are meant to be defined by the people of the generation living through that time, the culture today for instance is defined by Millennials & GenZ, not Boomers, all notions that boomers had or still have are being slowly redesigned, same way when GenZ is on a wheelchair, the Gen Beta can define the culture. Worrying about your [heritage](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DXSm5t_ANcF/) when you yourself have got some good 40 years of adult life left is bonkers.

When a system’s symbols are decoupled from its effects on humans, respect becomes performative for instance in India, touching the feet of old people is (or maybe was) considered part of our culture but can’t I disrespect an old man while touching his feet? That’s absurd, no? The act itself has no value, the intent does, do you know what’s more important than touching feet? Making sure old people get their pensions, do you know how much of a lucky draw that is in India?

The only thing intersubjective beliefs are good for anyways is to be documented in History books, so that we can learn what stupid shit we used to pull off back in the day. All past generations of all nations in the world have done some stupid thing, stuff that is evil, stuff which would be absurd today.

Art, however, operates differently from institutional systems. There’s absolutely no reason a curious Chinese citizen can’t learn *Bharatanatyam*, or a Russian native can’t learn to recite an African song, especially, especially if they have the conviction. An art form will preserve itself because it's a creative self-expression, possibly unique to humans, it's inherently selfish, people do it for themselves, to express, to connect. What if no-one wants to learn a dying art form? Document that and move on, it will be rediscovered. What’s worse? Letting an art form die or expecting people to express themselves in a way they don’t wanna be?

Another messy question is where do we draw the line in not respecting a system which is primarily a group of people holding a problematic belief? 

One way to approach this is how modern psychology states that explanation of someone’s position is not an excuse for their behaviour (your trauma doesn’t justify your mistake, it only explains it). The primary example of this is you can support freedom of speech against the government and at the same time, exclude people from systems that use derogatory language toward other fellow humans, mostly as a way to insult. Modern forms of accountability, such as cancel culture, emerge precisely when a system’s internal norms fail to exclude harmful behavior.

The dilemma becomes even messier when we are the ones who created the system in the first place. System leaders naturally suffer from things like ownership bias, [IKEA effect](https://til.bhupesh.me/psychology/ikea-effect), treating the failures of people within their system as individual missteps rather than flaws in the blueprint itself.

Bad systems are also very good at justifying their own expansion by pointing to threats generated by the very environment they have fostered. In the fictional *Terminator* series, Skynet had to build humanoid Terminators as a response to how humans changed their combat strategies, primary use-case was infiltration. Now you tell me, Does the Terminator exist because Resistance exists?

Similarly in the real world, every argument that relies on "my country first" or "my country only" speaks from a colonial mindset. Whether a nation is historically an imperial power or simply aspires to be one, such slogans reduce human empathy to arbitrary borders. If you claim that only care about a specific subset of humans, you sir very much lack emotional intelligence and have been indoctrinated successfully. To me that is a very disgusting mindset.

One of my favorite emergent properties of a system is resilience. If systems are resilient, they will survive and recover. This goes for both bad & good systems, now how we define what’s bad and good will once again rely on the generation of that time, that’s why intrusions to systems like protests, coups, competition, progressive ideas are important. If systems don’t survive, it will be ignorant to not break them down, redesign & reform on a continuous basis, the same way humans are expected to grow with time.

Over centuries, humanity has had ample opportunity to outgrow rigid structures, including legacy religious and institutional frameworks. Whether you feel [alienated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marx%27s_theory_of_alienation) or trapped in an [iron cage](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.276060), build up systemic literacy, stop sucking up to systems, we build them to serve us, no system is inherently perfect, because they are being designed by imperfect beings. I can expect you to be imperfect but not the systems you & I build, strive for constant reforms, and work together to figure out how.
