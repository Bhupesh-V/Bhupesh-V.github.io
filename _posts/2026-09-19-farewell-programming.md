Close to 9 years I have practiced programming as a hobby, today I'm deciding to put an indefinite pause on it.

My free time outside of work hours will no longer be filled with thoughts about the computer, whatever it can or will do for me. I'm not gonna lie, being a programmer was my identity for years, sure I changed words to describe it but I've now reached the point of disillusionment, two reasons that made me take this decision:

1. It does not give me the same joy as it used to when I was a young lad; all the wonder has diminished. For sometime, the void was filled, but as I grew up, I realized the void was as big as a motherfucking pool.
2. Majority people in this industry are imprudent to a point it becomes excruciatingly hard to give a fuck about anything we do. Surrounded by all these smart people working on "world changing" innovation, I've not once spotted an iota of soul. Maybe that's my fault, if not, you will see them obsess over solving problems that literally don't matter irl.

Just the other day, I was watching _Nomadland (2020)_, which features a group of old wise fellas sharing their nomad life, mostly grieving and finding reasons to live again. Now I may not be able to pack my bags literally, I can atleast stop doing something which no longer gives me a reason to spend my creative energy on or whatever is left of it.

The skills I've acquired over these years will never leave me, after all I'm writing this via archaic text editor, besides I still have to feed myself. There was a time when I stopped playing with my toys when I got access to [my first PC](/my-first-loop/), today it feels the same. The glasses I've been wearing for so long have gotten multiple scratches over the years; what seems definite now, though, is that they are broken.

What will I do next? Writing seems like a very obvious choice; it's clearly a superset of programming. Plus, humans have the tendency to appreciate good stories, we have been telling them for centuries. I can spend more time watching movies and reading books and use that as an excuse to research, to kindle my inspiration which ultimately may teach me more about the world. Nothing of that sort exists with programming. Somewhere in all the upcoming essays & fiction on this site, there will be a part of me expressing itself.

Since it's a very old hobby, there are chances that you may see me relapsing. Don't hesitate to remind me with the phrase: _What did you write yesterday, Dan Brown?_

If joy has a habit of returning, then I’m hopeful that this enlarged new void will be filled by this new hobby. If not, I will see you all with an updated perspective. It's time for a new prescription for my glasses; it's time to learn how to swim.
